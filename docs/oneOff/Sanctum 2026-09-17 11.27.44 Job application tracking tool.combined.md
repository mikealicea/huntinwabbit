# Job application tracking tool

## Summary

### TL;DR
You want to build a tool that parses job links, manages resume and cover letter versions, ranks and categorizes positions, tracks application status, builds company profiles with interview research, and integrates LinkedIn for referrals.

### Dashboard and link parsing

You want to drop a job link into a dashboard and have it parsed automatically, with all requirements laid out. This applies to both companies you're interested in and specific positions.

### Resume and cover letter management

You want to manage multiple resume versions so you can send the right one or craft a resume for a particular role. This extends to managing cover letters and similar application materials.

### Ranking, categorization, and status

You want to rank positions using simple heuristics—clicking, upvoting, or something similar. You also want to categorize roles (e.g., throwaway applications, trying to get experience) and track status (applied, pending, etc.).

### Company profiles and postmortems

You want to create company profiles to research their interview loops and similar details. Afterward, you want to do a postmortem on the process and what you were tracking.

### LinkedIn network integration

You want to add your entire LinkedIn network to the tool so you can find people within one or two degrees of separation for referrals.

## Simplicity vs. comprehensiveness
The tool envisions a wide feature set—link parsing, resume management, ranking, categorization, status tracking, company profiles, postmortems, and LinkedIn integration—while the ranking interaction is explicitly meant to stay simple (simple heuristics, clicking or upvoting). This raises a design question: which features need the same lightweight treatment to keep the tool usable, and which warrant more structure?
